## My First Half Marathon

This day was especially meaningful to me and my family. Seven years earlier, on this same date, my wife and our first child, Sofian, arrived in Taiwan for the first time. Since then, we have not had the chance to return to Egypt. I wanted to celebrate our shared perseverance by reaching my longest distance and completing the half-marathon challenge on that anniversary.

The timing made the achievement even more special. On November 1, I completed my 50-day, 5 km challenge without interruption. I planned November 2 as a rest day so I could recover and prepare properly for the half marathon. Finishing it the next day felt like a beautiful continuation of that achievement, and I dedicated it to my wife as a small thank-you for standing beside me throughout this uncertain journey.

During the run, I kept thinking about the memories we have built and everything we have faced together. At first, we imagined that Taiwan would be a two-year stop while I completed my master's degree, followed by a move to the United States or Europe for my PhD. How naive that plan seems now. Reality hit us hard from the first month after my family arrived. We were surprised by the difficulties of life in Taiwan and by how demanding it could be to build a life here with a family, but we decided to face it together.

In those early days, we even had to sell one of my wife's gold jewelry pieces to cover some of the costs of settling down and paying the rental deposit for an apartment outside the university. A few months after my family arrived, the COVID-19 pandemic changed the world. We often think about how difficult our reunion might have been if we had waited longer before traveling. Later, many of our plans fell through. We could not find many suitable programs in the United States, some universities suspended admissions, and we could not afford to submit multiple paid applications to the programs that remained open. Eventually, we accepted the opportunity to continue my studies in Taiwan in the same laboratory.

Many other unexpected events and hardships followed. I am grateful that my wife was with me through all of them and that we found comfort in each other. I had grown up facing difficulties and had developed the determination to pursue my ambitions, but my wife was far less prepared for the challenges that came our way. She endured one difficult experience after another and showed a remarkable perseverance that revealed her true strength. She proved to me that she is made of something as strong and precious as a hard stone.

By the grace of Allah, we have made it through seven years in Taiwan and raised our two children here, far from our wider families. We continue to hold on to our faith that Allah will help us through whatever hardships and new experiences come next. The best is still ahead of us.

This achievement could not have happened without my wife's presence and support. I pray that Allah preserves her, grants her a long life beside me, and gives us both patience and perseverance throughout this journey. Ameen.
