This completed the full challenge. Done!

## What This Challenge Taught Me

This challenge was a great experience. It was a first of its kind for me, and it taught me a lot about myself, the strength of the mind, and how to develop the mindset needed to achieve goals that initially seem difficult. It also made me think about how to transfer what I learned from this experience to my studies, my personal challenges, and any future attempt to build a habit or make a meaningful change. The same principles that helped me keep running can be useful far beyond running.

The challenge came with several obstacles. Running continuously made my back muscles ache in a way I had never experienced before, and I also had problems with my hip flexors and right thigh joint. I started seeking medical assistance during the challenge and began rehabilitation therapy, along with more frequent hot compresses and stretching. My health improved a lot, and I learned that persistence also means paying attention to your body and taking the right steps to recover.

At the core of the challenge, keeping a stable running schedule was almost impossible. My days were busy and unpredictable, but I gradually became better at fitting the run into my day and doing it whatever it took. Day 41 was a particularly memorable example. A typhoon was passing nearby Taiwan, and while I was riding home on my scooter, the winds were strong enough to push the scooter and make me lose control several times. It was a day off across the country, and I thought I should get home and complete my run before the weather made it impossible.

I was glad I made that decision. The rain and wind almost stopped when I arrived home, so I could finish the run safely. By the evening, the rain had become heavy again and the winds were much stronger. The next day was the weekend, but I kept delaying my run until the wind and rain stopped completely, almost in the evening. Then, on the following day, my family had arranged an outing to Taipei Zoo, so there was no way to run early in the morning. I even thought about running during the day while the children rested somewhere inside the zoo, but my wife did not like that idea. The only way to avoid missing the run was to return home and go out immediately. After almost twelve hours of riding buses and walking around Taipei Zoo, it felt especially satisfying to complete that run that night.

This kind of consistent training built a mental muscle that I believe I can transfer to future challenges. When you are deeply interested in something and believe in the value it adds to your life, commitment becomes much easier to maintain, even when the conditions are far from ideal.

For now, I am giving myself one day off to recover and prepare for another challenge starting on November 3: extending my 5 km running challenge to 100 days. Let’s see how it goes!
