I think I ran my first unofficial sub-30-minute 5K! 😄

Before you ask for the screenshot: the app says **30:17**. It was my second-fastest recorded 5K, just six seconds behind my best. But between the rush-hour traffic, repeated stops, and hotter-than-expected weather, I finished believing I could have gone under 30 minutes without the interruptions.

That is still a belief, not a recorded result. The official sub-30 will have to wait for another day! But it made me want to share the story behind this run—and what has helped me become more consistent.

When I started writing this, I had completed 32 days. The challenge is still going: by publication, I had reached **Day 34** of my 50-day goal.

## Running for Years, but Not Consistently

I have been running for years, but regular running is a much more recent chapter. From May 24, I started aiming for three or four runs a week, with a big goal in mind: preparing for my first marathon.

Now, please don't imagine someone particularly slim and athletic writing this. People keep asking me, “With all this running, why doesn't it show on you?”

Believe me, I am as puzzled as you are! :D

As I planned longer runs, I also found myself eating more carbohydrates to keep up with them. Then an injury interrupted my running shortly before my trip to the USA in early August. After I returned, several celebrations followed, and the weight I had been losing started creeping back up.

I felt I needed a different strategy.

## The Idea Came from My Lab Mate

My Chinese lab mate had a story that stayed with me. He used to weigh more than 90 kg; now he is around 60 kg and runs full marathons.

How had he started? With 3 km a day, for a few months, before gradually stretching those runs into longer distances. Over time, his speed and stamina developed too.

His experience gave me an idea: instead of always thinking about the next long run, what if I focused on showing up consistently?

With a little David Goggins-style enthusiasm, I set myself a challenge: **5 km every day for 30 days. No skipped days!**

That was the commitment I made to myself, with years of running behind me. It wasn't a training plan I had tested for anyone else.

## Thirty Days—With a Family and a PhD

My timing was interesting, to put it politely.

I started on **September 13**, after my children's school year had begun and my own semester was underway. The plan was to run before the family woke up, get home, shower, wash my running clothes, and be ready before breakfast.

I promised my wife and asked her to bear with me for just 30 days. :D

Of course, surprises never wait for an invitation. Wet-lab experiments joined the schedule, alongside family duties and everything else that already needed doing.

Some mornings, I could barely pull myself out of bed. On those days, I would drop off the children, head to the university in my running clothes, leave my bag at the lab, and go straight out for a run on campus.

That solved the timing problem. It did not always solve the weather problem!

By the time I reached campus, it could already be very hot. On some of those runs, I returned with my face looking like a piece of freshly cut liver. Not exactly the fresh, energetic look I had been hoping for! 😅

On hot days, waiting until near sunset became the more practical option.

## What I Would Share with Someone Starting Their Own Challenge

For me, the useful lesson has been to make the commitment clear while staying flexible about where it fits into the day. Before breakfast, after the school drop-off, or near sunset—the schedule had to work around real life.

My lab mate's story inspired me, but my challenge was my own. I would share that idea of finding a manageable starting point, rather than promise that copying my daily distance will produce the same result.

For now, I have a recorded **30:17**, an encouraging trend, and a little unfinished business with that 30-minute mark.
