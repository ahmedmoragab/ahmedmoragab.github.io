Ahmed Mohamed Ragab

Computational Biology | Molecular Simulation | AI for Biology

Hello! I’m Ahmed M. Ragab, an Egyptian computational biologist and PhD candidate at the Institute of Bioinformatics and Structural Biology, National Tsing Hua University (NTHU), Taiwan.

Research

My research lies at the intersection of computational biology, structural biology, and molecular biophysics. I study –1 programmed ribosomal frameshifting (–1PRF), a translation mechanism exploited by viruses such as SARS-CoV-2. I combine molecular dynamics simulations, molecular docking, bioinformatics, and experimental approaches to understand viral RNA dynamics and explore small molecules that can modulate frameshifting efficiency.

My Journey

I was the first in my family to reach university—and even to enter secondary school. In Egypt, the Scientific Thanawya Amma is the science track of general secondary education, which can open the door to university programs such as medicine, pharmacy, and science. My interest in science began early; people in my neighborhood nicknamed me Ahmed Zewail, after the Egyptian chemist who received the 1999 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his studies of chemical reactions using femtosecond spectroscopy.

University placement in Egypt was based on results accumulated across the second and third years of secondary school. In my second year, I scored almost 97%, the second-highest result in my school. In my final year, I earned nearly full marks in most subjects, but scored 40 out of 50 in physics. In the days before that exam, my mother’s health problems meant several trips back and forth to see doctors, and I believe the worry and exhaustion affected me. My combined score led me to the Faculty of Science at Cairo University.

What once felt as if a score had decided my future became one of the best things that could have happened to me. At Cairo, I rediscovered myself—and met my beloved wife. I also found myself following a path that reminded me of Ahmed Zewail’s: he studied in the Faculty of Science at Alexandria University before becoming a Nobel laureate.

I earned my BSc in Zoology/Chemistry at Cairo University in 2015. In 2019, I moved from Egypt to Taiwan after receiving a full scholarship from Taiwan’s Ministry of Education to pursue my master’s degree at NTHU. I completed my MSc in Bioinformatics and Structural Biology in January 2022, supervised by Prof. Lee-Wei Yang and Prof. Jin-Der Wen, and began my PhD at the same institute in February 2022 under Prof. Lee-Wei Yang.

My path into computational research was not a straightforward one. I started with a background in chemistry and zoology, moved into molecular biology and experimental research, and eventually found my way into bioinformatics, molecular simulation, and programming. More recently, I have become increasingly interested in machine learning and artificial intelligence and how they can be used to understand complex biological systems and accelerate scientific discovery.

Science has also given me opportunities to see more of the world. I have presented my research at international conferences and scientific meetings in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, and the United States, experiences that have shaped both my scientific interests and my perspective on what it means to be part of an international research community.

Beyond the Lab

Outside the lab, I love running. For me, running is another way of challenging myself, staying consistent, and discovering what I can do with enough persistence. I recently completed a personal challenge of running 5 km every day for 30 consecutive days, and my next goal is completing a half marathon.

I created this website to document both sides of that journey: my development as a scientist and my growth as a person. Here you’ll find my research, publications, writings, ongoing projects and challenges, achievements, and some of the things I learn along the way.
