Hello! I'm **Ahmed M. Ragab**, an Egyptian computational biologist and PhD candidate at the **Institute of Bioinformatics and Structural Biology, National Tsing Hua University (NTHU), Taiwan**.

## Research

My research brings together computational biology, structural biology, and molecular biophysics. I study **−1 programmed ribosomal frameshifting (−1PRF)**, a translation mechanism exploited by viruses such as SARS-CoV-2. I combine molecular dynamics simulations, molecular docking, bioinformatics, and experimental approaches to understand viral RNA dynamics and explore small molecules that can modulate frameshifting efficiency.

## My Journey

I grew up with three siblings in a caring family with very limited financial means. Still, I was fortunate to have the chance to become the first in my family to attend secondary school and, later, university.

I had to start working at a young age, mainly during school holidays, to help ease the financial strain on my family. Over my school years, I took on more than ten different jobs. While many children spent their summer holidays playing, I often spent mine working to help cover the costs of going back to school.

Continuing my education beyond junior high school would not have been possible without substantial financial support. Alongside my mother, I took on much of the responsibility for running a small family business. Its earnings paid for much of my education, including the private lessons I attended while preparing for Egypt's Thanawya Amma secondary-school examinations. The business continued to support me through my university years.

Those early jobs also helped me grow as a person and shaped my understanding of the world around me. For instance, I worked in an office support role at a large mobile phone retailer, where I got an early look at workplace dynamics and how teams were managed. What I learned there still influences how I work with others and think about leadership today.

Alongside these responsibilities, my interest in science was growing. Although no one in my family was a scientist, I was known in my neighborhood for doing well at school from a young age. People even nicknamed me “Ahmed Zewail,” after the Egyptian Nobel Prize-winning chemist. That encouragement meant a great deal to me and stayed with me as I made my way toward university.

### Finding my direction at Cairo University

Despite my early interest in science, entering the Faculty of Science at Cairo University was not the path I had expected. My secondary-school results had changed my plans, and I arrived still feeling disappointed. Adjusting to a different style of learning and an English-language curriculum made that first year harder. I struggled with motivation, and my grades suffered, but chemistry and zoology stood out as the subjects I genuinely enjoyed and did best in.

During that first year, I also met the woman who would become my wife six years later. She helped me rethink my situation and gave me renewed motivation to do well in my studies. At the same time, I was becoming more interested in the connections between the two subjects I loved.

I kept wondering how the same atoms and molecules involved in reactions in a test tube could also come together within living cells to sustain life. What was it about their organization and interactions that made this possible? I became almost obsessed with understanding what I thought of as the “secret of life.”

That curiosity led me to specialize in zoology and chemistry. From my second year onward, my grades improved substantially. I was no longer simply trying to get through university. I had found subjects I wanted to keep exploring.

### I could not wait to study molecular biology

My zoology courses introduced me to biology at many scales, from animal adaptation and comparative anatomy to physiology and cell biology. As I learned more about living systems, I became increasingly curious about the molecules behind them. Proteins, in particular, captured my imagination. These tiny molecules were the workhorses of the cell, and I wanted to understand how they accomplished so much.

Molecular biology was scheduled for the fourth year of my degree, but I could not wait! With generous support from a family member who believed in me, I enrolled in an external molecular biology course that combined in-depth theory with hands-on laboratory training.

Through that course, I learned about the central dogma of molecular biology and the machinery behind DNA replication, repair, and gene expression. Alongside the theory, I gained practical experience in molecular cloning, DNA and RNA extraction, and conventional and real-time PCR. I also prepared my first DNA sequencing library. I was beginning to learn how to investigate the processes that had fascinated me in the classroom.

By the time I graduated in 2015 with a **BSc in Zoology/Chemistry**, I was among the top students in my cohort. I left Cairo University with an interest in molecular biology that I wanted to pursue further, but finding a way to continue my education would take time.

### Working toward postgraduate study abroad

After graduation, I needed to earn an income and prepare for marriage. My family's financial circumstances meant that postgraduate study had to be balanced with more immediate responsibilities.

The additional molecular biology training helped me secure a part-time technician position in a biomedical laboratory specializing in cancer biomarker testing. Working there strengthened my hands-on experience and later became an important part of my applications for postgraduate study abroad. I subsequently joined an established pharmaceutical company in Egypt as a medical representative.

My heart was divided between my wish to continue studying abroad and the need to keep earning a dependable income. Alongside work, I kept reading research articles, worked on my English, and took IELTS. With limited savings, I could only afford a small number of applications, so I focused on master's programs that might offer full funding.

Over the following years, I received an offer to study structural biology at Imperial College London, but without funding. An offer from Uppsala University in Sweden included a tuition-fee exemption, yet the living expenses were still beyond what I could afford. I was also shortlisted for Fulbright. None of these opportunities had yet made the move possible, but they gave me reason to keep trying. I could see that my applications were getting stronger each year.

After the birth of my first child, I felt time moving quickly and became even more determined to find a way forward. I considered studying in Spain or Germany with the help of a loan from a trusted mentor who was willing to support me. Around the same time, I learned about postgraduate programs in Taiwan and decided to apply to two universities there.

### Moving to Taiwan

I was fortunate to be admitted to both universities. I chose the **master's program in Bioinformatics and Structural Biology at NTHU**, beginning in the fall of 2019, with full funding from the university.

A few months later, before I traveled, I received more good news: I had been selected for a scholarship from **Taiwan's Ministry of Education**. Its more generous support made it possible for my family to accompany me.

After years of trying to make this happen, I was finally moving abroad to pursue my postgraduate studies, with my family beside me. It felt like the first real step toward the scientific career I had been dreaming of.

### Finding my way into computational biology

At NTHU, I began working with bioinformatics and molecular simulations, building on my background in experimental molecular biology. This also meant learning programming from scratch.

I loved seeing code I had written myself actually work. Being able to analyze my own data and prepare publication-ready figures saved a tremendous amount of time. That experience made me eager to learn more and see what else I could do with these tools.

Molecular simulation gave me another way to explore the movements and interactions of the molecules that had fascinated me as an undergraduate. My experimental background remained part of how I approached these questions, even as more of my work moved to the computer.

I completed my **MSc in Bioinformatics and Structural Biology in January 2022**, supervised by **Prof. Lee-Wei Yang and Prof. Jin-Der Wen**. In **February 2022**, I continued into a PhD at the same institute under **Prof. Lee-Wei Yang**.

As my programming skills have grown, I've also begun exploring machine learning and artificial intelligence. I enjoy being able to try these approaches myself and think about how they might help me answer biological questions.

### Sharing my research with a wider community

Alongside my research, I sought opportunities to present my work at conferences and scientific meetings. Through persistent applications and, in some cases, direct outreach to funding agencies and the people responsible for travel support, I have received **more than 14 travel grants**. I'm grateful to the people and organizations whose support made these trips possible.

These opportunities have taken me to meetings in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, and the United States. They have allowed me to share my work, learn from other researchers, and take part in experiences I once could not have imagined.

One particularly meaningful moment was giving a **15-minute platform talk at the 2026 Biophysical Society Annual Meeting**. For someone who had once wondered whether studying abroad would ever be financially possible, being there to present my own research meant a great deal.

Now, I'm working to bring my PhD to the finish line and preparing for the next stage of my research career!

## Beyond the Lab

**I love running**, too. I enjoy having a challenge to work toward outside research. I recently ran **5 km every day for 30 consecutive days**, and my next goal is to complete a **half marathon**.

More recently, I've become interested in leadership, entrepreneurship, and business development, returning in some ways to the questions about people and organizations that first interested me in my early jobs.

I created this website to share my work and some of the life around it. Alongside my research, publications, and projects, you'll find personal writing, challenges, and things I'm learning as I go.
